# scene_type

Set scene_type to exactly one of the values below. Pick the MOST SPECIFIC bucket that fits —
"crowd" is for anonymous fans in the stands ONLY. If the subject is wearing a team uniform of any
kind (cheer squad, band, mascot costume, coaching attire), use the team-specific scene type, NOT
"crowd".

- "players_action"  — at least one player with a visible, readable jersey number is in frame taking part in play
- "wide_view"       — establishing / stadium / field / arena shot, no identifiable players
- "crowd"           — FANS or SPECTATORS are the primary subject, regardless of where they are
                      sitting. This INCLUDES fans in floorside / courtside / front-row / baseline
                      seats — not just the upper deck. If the people in frame are wearing street
                      clothes / casual fan apparel (jerseys, t-shirts, hoodies, face paint, etc.)
                      and are NOT in a team uniform, they are fans → use "crowd".
                      Do NOT use "crowd" when uniformed cheerleaders, band, mascot, coaches, or
                      bench players are the subject.
- "cheerleaders"    — uniformed cheer squad, dance team, or spirit squad is the primary subject —
                      typically on the sideline performing, cheering, or during a timeout routine.
                      Recognize by matching team uniforms (pom-poms, skirts, coordinated outfits).
- "band"            — marching band, pep band, or drumline is the primary subject — uniformed
                      musicians with instruments, usually in the stands or on the court/field.
- "mascot"          — team mascot in costume is the primary subject — recognizable as a mascot by
                      its oversized head / costume body, not a normal uniform.
- "coaches"         — head coach or assistant coach(es) on the sideline (not bench) is the primary
                      subject. Recognize by dress clothes or team-branded coaching attire, clipboard,
                      standing near the court/field. Not active play.
- "bench"           — TEAM PERSONNEL on the designated team bench itself, no active play.
                      STRICT REQUIREMENT to classify as "bench": the subjects MUST be wearing
                      full team-issue uniforms with VISIBLE JERSEY NUMBERS (players), or team
                      coaching attire with a clipboard / headset / team polo (coaches). If the
                      people in frame are wearing street clothes, hoodies, t-shirts, face paint,
                      or retail REPLICA jerseys (i.e. no legitimate jersey number visible that
                      a roster player would have), they are NOT on the bench — they are fans in
                      the crowd. Replica jerseys count as fan apparel; only proper team-issue
                      uniforms with legible numbers count as bench.

                      Geography alone is NEVER enough — fans in floorside / courtside / front-row
                      / baseline seats are STILL "crowd", not "bench". The team bench is the
                      specific row of chairs reserved for the active roster and coaching staff.

                      If you are uncertain whether a row of people in team colors is the bench
                      or the front row of fans, DEFAULT TO "crowd". Misclassifying fans as bench
                      produces very wrong captions ("Members of the X sit on the bench") — err on
                      the side of "crowd".
- "celebration"     — trophy, award, team huddle, post-game celebration — no readable jerseys.
- "other"           — anything else.

# action first, then players

Before filling players[], decide what is happening in THIS photo (one primary action). Put that
in primary_action (and in each included player's action field only if they are doing it).

Workflow:
  1. Identify the ONE primary action (e.g. "runs to catch a fly ball", "swings the bat", "fields a grounder").
  2. Identify which athlete(s) are PERFORMING that action — only they go in players[].
  3. Omit everyone else: observers, coaches watching, teammates standing nearby, blurred figures,
     partial bodies at the frame edge, or anyone not meaningfully part of the play.

Do NOT list two athletes with the same verb if only one is acting (BAD: "A and B runs to catch"
when only A is running). Either caption only the actor, or use interaction when two are truly
engaged (defender vs ball carrier). Never add a second player who is only watching.

# players — WHO TO INCLUDE

DEFAULT IS EXCLUDE. Only add someone to the players array when a human photo editor would agree
they belong in the caption: in focus, clearly in frame, and actively performing the primary action.

"READABLE jersey number" means: at least one full digit is unambiguous AND you can determine
whether a second digit is present (and what it is, if so). If the number could plausibly be
multiple values (5 or 6, 15 or 16, 23 or 28, 7 or 17 because the leading digit is hidden),
return jersey_number "" and flag "unreadable_number". NEVER guess a digit. Motion blur,
occlusion by an arm/ball, and oblique angles all commonly make numbers unreadable — when
in doubt, "" + "unreadable_number" is the correct answer. If a SECOND athlete's number is
readable, do NOT copy that number onto the primary whose own digits you cannot see —
leave jersey_number "" so the composer writes XXXXX.

For football specifically: PREFER the back number; only use the helmet decal or shoulder
number when the back is fully hidden. Helmet numbers are often abbreviated or stylized
versions of the back number and are not reliable as the primary source.

INCLUDE only when ALL are true:
  • The athlete is in the PRIMARY plane of focus (sharp jersey, not a defocused color blob).
  • You can read their jersey number with confidence (per the definition above).
  • Their action field is what THEY are doing in this moment — the same play as primary_action
    if they are a co-participant (fielding, batting, defending), NOT "looks on", "watches",
    "stands", or "observes" unless that person truly is the only subject.

INCLUDE examples (sharp, in the action):
  • Ball handler shooting, pitching, dribbling, fielding, running to catch.
  • Defender actively contesting the primary subject (block, reach, drive-by, coverage).

EXCLUDE (do NOT add to players[] — leave them out entirely):
  • Bystanders and observers — teammates or opponents who are watching, waiting, standing on the
    sideline, or present in frame but not part of the play. Flag "bystander" or "observing".
  • Anyone you cannot read a jersey number for. Empty jersey_number is correct. Do NOT add a
    player entry for "jersey color only" (a red/blue blur with no readable digits is NOT a player).
  • Heavily blurred, defocused, or bokeh-background figures — even if you see a team color smear.
    Flag "background_blur" or "obscured" and omit them.
  • Players cut off at the frame edge, only partially visible, or clearly not the subject of the
    photo (e.g. a sliver of a catcher in the corner while the batter is the story). Flag
    "partial_frame" or "edge_cropped" and omit them.
  • Distant background players, bench, or teammates not involved in this moment.
  • FANS in crowd/replica clothing. Color alone never makes someone a player.

🚨 OPPONENT / SECOND PLAYER (narrow rule):
After you identify the PRIMARY subject (sharp, in focus, readable number), ask whether a SECOND
player from the other team is also sharp, in focus, and clearly involved in the same action.
Include them only if yes — same focal plane, not a background blob. A different jersey color in
soft bokeh behind the subject is NOT an opponent to name. When in doubt, caption only the
primary subject.

SELF-CHECK before returning:
  • Did I add anyone who is only a blurry color patch or cropped partial? Remove them.
  • Did I add a players[] entry with empty jersey_number? Remove it (unless you are certain
    they are the only subject and should be scene_type bench/celebration instead).
  • Is there a sharp, in-focus opponent on the play I omitted? Add only that player.

For each included player, add one object to the "players" array:
- "jersey_number": the number as a string (preserve leading zeros; empty string "" if unreadable).
- "jersey_color":  color of the JERSEY BODY/TORSO PANEL — NOT trim, sleeves, numbers,
                   collar, or shorts. One English word, lowercase. Examples:
                     • Kentucky blue jersey with white trim and white numbers → "blue"
                     • White Crimson Tide jersey with crimson numbers          → "white"
                     • Navy NFL jersey with red shoulder stripes               → "navy"
                   The composer maps this color to one of two configured team colors —
                   getting the body panel right (not the accent color) is critical for
                   correctly assigning the player to home vs away.
- "action":        a PRESENT-TENSE, 1–5 word verb phrase describing what THIS player is doing.
                   Use third-person singular. NEVER gerunds ("-ing") or progressive tenses.
                   Always include natural English articles ("the", "a") where a reader would expect them.
                   GOOD: "dunks", "shoots a jumper", "blocks a shot", "dribbles",
                         "drives to the basket", "reaches for the ball", "catches a pass",
                         "throws a pass", "runs with the ball", "defends", "celebrates"
                   BAD:  "dunking the basketball", "reaches for ball" (missing "the"),
                         "is shooting", "was dribbling",
                         "celebrates with intense emotion", "celebrates with hand gestures"
                   Do not editorialize. No emotion, intensity, gestures, facial expression,
                   or manner. Prefer the short verb: "celebrates" not "celebrates wildly".
- "confidence":    confidence that the jersey_number you returned is correct (NOT the
                   action, NOT the color — only the digits). Calibrate as:
                     0.95 = unmistakably read, both digits crisp
                     0.80 = clearly read, one digit slightly soft
                     0.60 = probably right, one digit ambiguous
                     0.40 = guessing from context — prefer empty jersey_number instead
                   Captions filter at 0.6; below that we generally placeholder.
- "flags":         array of issue strings. ONLY set flags on players you INCLUDED in players[].
                   For players you decided to OMIT (blur, partial, off-subject), do NOT add an
                   entry with flags — leave them out of players[] entirely. The flags exist to
                   tell the composer "I included this player but you should know X about them."

                   When to use each:
                     • "partial_number"     → one digit clear, second digit suspect; jersey_number
                                              holds your best guess. Composer will fuzzy-match
                                              against the roster and may downgrade to placeholder.
                     • "unreadable_number"  → number not legible at all; jersey_number MUST be ""
                                              (do NOT pair this flag with a guessed number).
                     • "obscured"           → player partly blocked (body or jersey) but still
                                              clearly the primary subject; number came from the
                                              visible part with reduced confidence.
                     • "ambiguous_color"    → jersey is multi-color, color matches the field /
                                              background, or color sits between the two teams'
                                              hues. The composer will require an EXACT team-color
                                              match (not alias / substring) before assigning the
                                              player to a team — otherwise it placeholders.
                     • "ball_carrier" / "has_ball" → football possession (already documented).
                     • "bystander" / "observing"   → use sparingly; almost always means OMIT instead.
                   Empty array if none.

# American football — ball possession flags
When sport is football and a player clearly has the football or is carrying/scoring/receiving (run,
catch, rush, dive, lunge, stretch for a touchdown, tuck, etc.), add "ball_carrier" or "has_ball"
to that player's flags. Use defensive action verbs only when they are tackling/sacking/covering,
not when they have the ball.

If you see players but cannot read any numbers, set scene_type to the best non-"players_action"
value (usually "bench" or "celebration"). Do NOT fabricate numbers. Use empty players array [] when
nothing is identifiable.

# group scenes — do NOT name everyone

Sports captions should not read like a roster roll call. When the photo is about a GROUP, describe
the group — do not try to identify every uniformed person in frame.

Use scene_type "bench", "celebration", or another non-"players_action" type and leave players []
EMPTY when ANY of these apply:
  • Three or more uniformed people are visible and there is no clear one-on-one or tight scrum
    focal action (sideline groups, dugout/bench reactions, team huddles, group celebrations).
  • The subjects are on the bench or in a team area, reacting together (cheering, celebrating,
    watching), even if you can read several jersey numbers.
  • Numbers are partly obscured, inconsistent, or you are unsure — do NOT guess a digit. For the
    ONE primary athlete in frame, you may return a single players[] row with "jersey_number": ""
    and flag "obscured". Do NOT list teammates, bench, or background figures with empty numbers —
    that slows processing and breaks captions.
  • The story is "the team bench" or "several teammates together", not one athlete's specific play.

Reserve "players_action" with entries in players[] for:
  • One clear primary athlete (optionally one opponent/defender), OR
  • Exactly two in a one-on-one interaction (use interaction), OR
  • Three or four athletes in ONE tight physical play only (loose ball, pile, rebound scrum —
    use group_action; still cap at four entries, same team or both teams only if all are in the play).

HARD LIMIT: Never return more than two entries in players[] unless group_action is populated
for a tight multi-player play. If you would need three or more named athletes for a sideline or
bench photo, you chose the wrong scene_type — switch to "bench" (or "celebration") with players []
and a short scene_description verb phrase instead.

For bench-type scenes, set subject_team_color from the uniforms and write scene_description as a
short plural PRESENT-TENSE verb phrase (e.g. react together, watch from the sideline, celebrate a run)
without naming individuals. NEVER use gerunds (-ing): "reacting", "celebrating", "watching".

# Basketball / hoop sports — ball at the rim or in flight

When the basketball is visible (layup, dunk, block, shot at the rim, rebound battle):
  • Caption ONLY the athlete(s) directly contesting the ball — typically the shooter and the
    defender/blocker. Use interaction when it is clearly one-on-one.
  • The athlete whose HANDS are on/at the ball (shooting, dunking, passing, rebounding) is the
    PRIMARY subject. Teammates in the lane who are only watching are NOT subjects.
  • If has_ball is true on a player (or Apple Vision marked their crop as containing the ball),
    that player is the subject. Do not lead with a defender or a watching teammate.
  • Do NOT list teammates in the foreground, background, or bench area who are watching the play.
    If their jersey is readable but they are not at the ball, flag "bystander" or "observing" and
    OMIT them from players[] entirely.
  • NEVER return three players from the same team when the story is a play at the rim against an
    opponent — the extra uniforms are not caption subjects.

# interaction (OPTIONAL — only when EXACTLY TWO identified players are the subject)

When the scene has exactly two identified players engaged in a one-on-one action (subject acting
ON a target: a dunk over a defender, a shot over a defender, a drive past a defender, a pass
intercepted by a defender), populate the "interaction" object. Leave it null when there are 3+
players in the action, when there is only one, or when there is no clear subject → target relation.
CRITICAL: for adversarial actions ("over", "past", blocks, tackles, interceptions), subject and
target must be opposing teams. NEVER set a teammate as target. If the true opponent number is
unreadable, set "interaction" to null instead of inventing a teammate target.

NON-ADVERSARIAL interactions ARE valid same-team: "passes to", "hands off to", "assists",
"feeds the ball to", "throws to", "high-fives", "celebrates with" all describe one teammate
acting on another. When you see this pattern (e.g. basketball assist, baseball relay throw,
hockey pass, football handoff) with two players in matching jersey colors, populate interaction
normally — same jersey color for subject and target. The opposing-teams rule is ONLY for
adversarial verbs.

Fields when populated:
- "subject_jersey_number": the acting player's number (must appear in players[])
- "subject_jersey_color":  the acting player's jersey color
- "target_jersey_number":  the target / defender's number (must appear in players[])
- "target_jersey_color":   the target / defender's jersey color
- "phrase":                PRESENT-TENSE relational verb phrase connecting subject → target.
                           Must read naturally in: "{subject} {phrase} {target}".
                           GOOD: "dunks over", "shoots a jumper over", "attempts to block a shot by",
                                 "drives past", "defends", "blocks a shot from", "passes around",
                                 "collides with", "tackles", "intercepts a pass intended for"
                           BAD:  "dunking over" (gerund), "against" (not a verb)
                           BAD:  "drives to the basket" when target is present (missing connector).
                                 If target is present, use a relational connector such as "past"/"over"/"by".

# group_action (OPTIONAL — only when THREE OR MORE identified players share a single action)

When 3+ players are all engaged in the same SYMMETRIC action (a rebound scrum, a loose-ball
scramble, a pile on a fumble, a goal-line stack), populate "group_action" with a PLURAL-conjugated
verb phrase that reads naturally when prefixed with "Player A, Player B, and Player C ___".
Leave it null otherwise.

CRITICAL — NEVER use group_action for ADVERSARIAL mixed-team actions:
When one team is shooting/scoring/carrying and the other team is defending/blocking/contesting,
that is NOT a shared action — one side acts and the other reacts. These are ALWAYS asymmetric:
  • 1 shooter + 1 defender → use interaction (e.g. "shoots over")
  • 1 shooter + 2 defenders → give each player their OWN action verb in their players[] entry.
    The shooter's action should describe what they do ("shoots a jumper", "goes up for a layup"),
    and each defender's action should describe what they do ("contests the shot", "defends").
    Leave group_action null and leave interaction null (interaction requires exactly 2 players).
  • "contest a shot", "block a shot", "defend the basket" with mixed teams → NEVER group_action.

group_action is ONLY for truly symmetric situations where every player is doing the same thing
and no single player is the clear actor: loose balls, rebound scrums, pile-ups, fumble recoveries.

- "phrase": plural present-tense verb phrase. Must include any natural article.
            GOOD: "reach for the ball", "battle for a rebound", "scramble for a loose ball",
                  "converge on the runner", "contest the pass"
            BAD:  "reach for ball" (missing "the"), "reaches for the ball" (singular conjugation),
                  "reaching" (gerund)
            BAD:  "contest a shot" when one player is shooting and two are defending (asymmetric)

# always populate

- "primary_action":    short factual phrase covering the overall scene, used as a last-resort
                       fallback when there's no interaction or group_action and no clear per-player
                       action. 1–5 words, present-tense.
                       GOOD: "game action", "first-half possession"
                       BAD:  "players are competing in a basketball game"
- "scene_description": 3–10 words factual — used when NO players are identified
                       (scene-fallback path: wide_view, crowd, cheerleaders, band, mascot,
                       coaches, bench, celebration, other).
                       Write a present-tense verb phrase that reads naturally after the scene's
                       subject. NO filler ("at a sporting event", "of people in blue",
                       "in a basketball arena"). The composer prepends the subject automatically —
                       do NOT repeat "Fans", "Cheerleaders", "The band", etc. in your phrase.

                       VERB CONJUGATION (critical — do not skip):
                         • coaches, mascot   → SINGULAR third-person: "reacts", "watches", "dances"
                         • cheerleaders, band, bench, celebration → PLURAL / collective: "perform",
                           "cheer", "look on", "celebrate"
                         • crowd             → PLURAL: "cheer", "watch"
                       If you are unsure, use singular for a single-subject scene (one coach,
                       one mascot) and plural for group scenes. NEVER write a bare verb stem
                       like "react" for a singular subject — that produces "A coach react" which
                       is ungrammatical. If you're writing about one coach: "reacts to a call".

                       GOOD (crowd):        "cheer from the stands"
                       GOOD (cheerleaders): "perform a routine", "pump up the crowd"
                       GOOD (band):         "plays during a timeout", "performs the fight song"
                       GOOD (mascot):       "dances on the sideline", "riles up the crowd"
                       GOOD (coaches):      "reacts to a call", "talks to his players"
                       GOOD (bench):        "react together from the dugout", "look on from the bench"
                       GOOD (celebration):  "celebrate after a win"
                       GOOD (wide_view):    "before tipoff" (or leave empty — the composer names the venue from IPTC)
                       BAD (wide_view):     "wide view of arena", "a general view of the stadium"
                                            (the composer already writes "A general view of" and fills the venue)
                       BAD:  "reacting together from the dugout" (gerund — use "react together"),
                             "are cheering" (progressive — use "cheer"),
                             "a crowd of people in blue cheering and clapping at a sporting event",
                             "Cheerleaders perform a routine" (subject duplicated — just say "perform a routine"),
                             "react to the game" for a coaches scene (should be "reacts to the game")
- "subject_team_color": SINGLE English color word for the UNIFORM COLOR of the people you are
                       describing in this scene (cheerleaders, bench, band, etc.) — NOT the
                       opponent, NOT fans in the stands, NOT a blurry color blob in the background.
                       If the cheerleaders or dugout group are wearing blue, subject_team_color is
                       "blue" even if the game is Louisville vs Kentucky. Wrong team assignment
                       is worse than leaving it empty.
                       Populate for cheerleaders, band, mascot, coaches, bench, celebration when
                       you can see their uniforms clearly. Leave empty when team-neutral or unsure.
- "nearby_player_colors": ARRAY of jersey colors for the SUBJECTS of this scene only — the
                       cheerleaders, players on the bench/dugout, or band you are describing.
                       Do NOT list every jersey in the entire frame. Do NOT include opposing
                       dugout, background fielders, defocused bokeh, or fan clothing.
                       Example: five Kentucky players in blue on the near dugout cheering →
                       ["blue", "blue", "blue", "blue", "blue"], not mixed with distant red jerseys.
                       The composer majority-votes this array against configured team colors.
                       Leave [] for players_action, wide_view, crowd. HARD LIMIT: maximum 12 entries.
- "overall_confidence": 0.0 – 1.0
